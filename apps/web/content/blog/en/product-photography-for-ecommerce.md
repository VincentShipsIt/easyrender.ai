Good product photography for ecommerce comes from a setup you can repeat: one large soft light, a plain background, a camera on a tripod at the product's middle height, and the same shot list for every item. You do not need studio flashes. A window, a white foam board and a phone cover most small shops.

## What equipment do you need for ecommerce product photography?

A tripod matters more than the camera. It keeps every product at the same height and angle, so your category pages line up, and it lets you shoot in window light without blur.

A current phone is enough for listing photos. Use the 2x or 3x lens and step back. The wide 1x lens, used close, stretches whatever is nearest to it, so the front legs of a side table look longer than the back ones.

![The same side table drawn twice: through a 1x lens used close, its front legs look much longer than the back ones and the top narrows sharply; through a 2x or 3x lens from further back, the legs look even](diagram:lens-distance "Why to step back and zoom in. The closer the lens, the more the nearest parts grow. Drawn for this guide.")

The rest of the kit is cheap:

- A tripod with a phone clamp.
- A large white foam board, to bounce light into the shadow side.
- A plain backdrop: a white wall, a paper roll for small items, a clean painted floor for furniture.
- Masking tape, to mark where the tripod and the product stand so every shot matches.

## Product photography lighting: how do you set it up?

Use one big, soft source. A window with no direct sun on it, on a bright overcast day, is the cheapest softbox you will find.

Put the window at about 45 degrees to the front of the product and the white board on the opposite side. Then switch off the ceiling lights. Mixing warm bulbs with daylight gives you a white sofa that is blue on one side and yellow on the other.

Lock exposure and focus before you shoot. On an iPhone, press and hold on the product until AE/AF Lock appears, and every frame in the series comes out the same.

## Product photography background: white or not?

Use plain white or light grey for the main image. It keeps a catalogue consistent, and Amazon requires white for the main image of most products.

Save textured or styled backgrounds for the context shots further down the gallery. A linen sofa against a busy wallpaper reads as a room, not a product.

## Which angles does every product need?

Keep one order for every product. Buyers learn where to look, and you stop forgetting the back.

1. Main image: the whole product on a plain background, usually at a three-quarter angle.
2. Straight front.
3. Side profile, which shows depth.
4. Back, especially for furniture that might stand away from a wall.
5. Details: the material, joints, handles, a label or maker's mark.
6. Context: the piece in a room, or next to an object everyone knows the size of.

The three-quarter main image does the most work. It shows the front and one side at once, so the shopper reads the shape from a thumbnail.

## What do Amazon and Shopify ask for?

[Amazon's seller guide to product photos](https://sell.amazon.com/blog/product-photos) asks for a pure white background (RGB 255, 255, 255) on the main image, with the product filling 85% or more of the frame. It recommends images larger than 1,000 pixels so shoppers can zoom.

[Shopify's guide to product media](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/product-media/product-media-types) says 2048 x 2048 pixels usually displays best for square images. It also asks for one aspect ratio across a collection, so grids stay tidy. Both rules change, so check the pages before a big shoot.

## Furniture photography: what changes?

A sofa does not fit on a paper roll. Shoot it against a plain wall with clear floor in front, and leave space on every side so you can crop to any ratio later.

Put the camera at roughly the middle height of the piece, around seat height for a chair. Shooting down from standing height makes a sideboard look short and squat.

![Side view of a sideboard and two camera positions: one at the middle height of the piece looking level, one at standing eye height looking down at its top](diagram:camera-height "Level with the middle of the piece, the proportions hold. From eye height the camera sees the top and the front shrinks. Drawn for this guide.")

On used or vintage furniture, photograph every flaw close up and in good light. A clear photo of a scratch now saves an argument after delivery. [How to sell vintage furniture online](/learn/sell-vintage-furniture-online) covers the rest of the listing.

## What are the common mistakes?

<!-- IMAGE PENDING: a real pair shot by the team of the same piece, once under mixed light (window plus warm ceiling bulbs) and once in window light only, to show the colour cast. Do not use a stock photo. -->

Mixed light is the most common one: daylight from the window plus warm bulbs overhead. After that come crooked horizons and a busy backdrop that competes with the product.

Heavy colour edits are the last. They turn a bottle-green velvet chair into a different green. Edit for accuracy, not mood, and hold the real fabric next to your screen before you upload.

## FAQ

### Can you take ecommerce product photos with a phone?

Yes, a recent phone on a tripod, in soft window light, produces listing photos that are good enough for most marketplaces and stores. Use the 2x or 3x lens to avoid distortion, lock exposure and focus, and shoot every product from the same marked spot. The light and the background matter more than the camera.

### What is the best background for product photography?

Plain white or light grey for the main image, because Amazon requires white and it keeps a catalogue consistent. For furniture, a plain painted wall and a clean floor work better than a paper roll. Keep textured or styled backgrounds for the context shots further down the gallery, where the room is the point.

### How many photos should each product have?

Six to eight covers most products: a main image, front, side, back, two or three details and one in context. Furniture benefits from the full set because buyers cannot touch it before they order. A simple item like a pillar candle may only need three or four, as long as the main image is strong.

### What are Amazon's white background rules?

Amazon's seller guide asks for a pure white background, RGB 255, 255, 255, on the main image, with the product filling 85% or more of the frame. Images larger than 1,000 pixels let shoppers zoom, and the extra images can show the product in use. Amazon updates these rules, so check its page before a large shoot.

### Should you edit product photos?

Lightly. Straighten the horizon, crop to your standard ratio, correct the white balance and clean dust off the backdrop, but leave the product's colour and its flaws alone. The photo is a promise about what arrives, and an edit that buyers notice on delivery costs more than it ever gained you.
