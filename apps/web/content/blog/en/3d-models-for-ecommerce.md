A 3D model for ecommerce is a file, usually GLB or USDZ, that lets shoppers spin a product on screen and, on a phone, see it in their room with AR. Stores get them in four ways: from a 3D artist, from the manufacturer's CAD files, by scanning the product, or with AI from a single photo.

## What does a 3D model add to a product page?

A photo shows the view you chose. A 3D model lets the shopper choose: the back of a bookcase, the underside of a coffee table, how thick the cushion on a lounge chair really is.

On iPhone and iPad, a USDZ file opens in AR Quick Look and places the object in the room through the camera. On Android, Google's Scene Viewer does the same with a GLB.

AR shows how a piece looks in a room. Whether it also appears at its true size depends on the file carrying correct dimensions, and many models do not.

## Which file formats does a store need?

GLB for the web and Android, USDZ for AR on Apple devices. Most platforms that support 3D take one or both.

Shopify accepts 3D models as product media in the admin, in the same place you add photos. WooCommerce has no 3D support built in, so stores add a plugin, often one built on model-viewer, Google's open-source web component for showing GLB files.

Keep the files light. A product model has to load on a phone over mobile data, and each platform publishes its own size limit.

## How do stores get 3D models?

### Hire a 3D artist

An artist builds the model from your photos, drawings and measurements. It is the most accurate route and the slowest. Freelance marketplaces such as Fiverr and Upwork list product modellers, and quotes vary with the complexity of the piece.

### Use the manufacturer's CAD files

If you sell your own designs, the factory may already have CAD or SketchUp files. They are accurate but rarely ready for the web: they usually need real textures and a lighter version of the geometry before they load well on a page.

### Scan the product

Photogrammetry apps like Polycam and RealityScan build a model from many photos taken around the object. This suits pieces you have in hand, in a space where you can walk all the way round them.

### Generate it from one photo with AI

Image-to-3D tools such as easyrender, Meshy and Tripo predict the hidden sides from a single picture. It is the only route when you have a supplier photo and no access to the item. The trade-off is that the back is an estimate, so you check it before you keep the model.

## How do you choose for a whole catalogue?

Start with the products where depth matters: an armchair, a sideboard, a floor lamp with a shaped shade. Flat items like a framed print gain little from 3D.

Then test before you commit. Run one product through two routes, for example a phone scan and an image-to-3D model of the same table lamp, and compare them on a phone. That tells you more than any feature list.

For a large catalogue, look at bulk options and pricing per model. easyrender lets you drop a set of catalogue photos into a library, check a free preview for each, and build only the ones that look right.

## A real example: a walnut sideboard

A shop selling a walnut sideboard has three supplier photos: front, three-quarter and a close-up of the brass handles. The three-quarter shot is the one to use for image-to-3D, because it shows depth and one end panel.

Expect the doors, legs and wood grain to read well. Expect the brass handles to come out softer than in the photo, and look closely at the back, which the tool has never seen.

Then upload the GLB with your platform's own tools, such as Shopify's product media, and open the page on an iPhone and on an Android phone before you publish.

## What are the limits?

A model generated from one photo shows shape and materials, not measurements. Keep the dimensions in the product text, and do not rely on AR to tell a shopper whether a piece will fit.

Transparent and reflective products, like a glass vase or a chrome lamp, are hard for every method. Fine print and small logos come out soft.

3D does not replace photos. Platforms still use still images for search results, ads and category grids, so the [product photography for ecommerce](/learn/product-photography-for-ecommerce) basics still apply.

## FAQ

### Do I need 3D models for my online store?

Not for every product. 3D helps most where shoppers want to see depth and the back: chairs, sofas, sideboards, lamps. For flat or very simple items, good photos do the job. Start with a handful of products where buyers ask for more angles, and judge from there rather than converting everything at once.

### How much does a 3D model for ecommerce cost?

It depends on the route. Artists quote per model by complexity, scanning apps usually charge a subscription, and AI tools charge per model or per month. On easyrender the preview is free and a finished model costs 80 credits ($0.80), as listed on the pricing page in November 2026.

### Can shoppers see a 3D product in AR?

Yes, on most recent phones: iPhones and iPads use AR Quick Look with a USDZ file, and Android phones use Google's Scene Viewer with a GLB. The model appears in the room through the camera. It only appears at true size if the file was made with correct measurements.

### Which platforms support 3D product models?

Shopify supports 3D models as product media, while WooCommerce needs a plugin. On a custom site, Google's model-viewer component shows a GLB with a short snippet of code. Most marketplaces do not accept 3D files from sellers, so check the seller rules before you plan around one.
